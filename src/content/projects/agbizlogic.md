## Abstract

AgBizLogic helps growers and landowners reason about the financial and environmental consequences of farm decisions. Our Oregon State capstone team explored how conversational assistance and clearer workflows could make those tools easier to use and trust. The deliverable was a research-backed design proposal and supporting materials, rather than a production AI system.

## Context

The project documentation describes growers struggling to return to complex workflows after periods of non-use, navigate dense manuals, and assess AI-generated guidance. This made both usability and trust central design questions. The intended audiences included growers, landowners, extension agents, students, and researchers.

## My role

I served as group lead. Sam Rossback and Carly Sherman were UX researchers, and Raahi Vaidya was the AI researcher. The interviews, prototype, testing, synthesis, and recommendations described here are team deliverables; the public project record does not assign every task to an individual.

## Approach

The team moved from stakeholder engagement and problem definition in fall 2024 to prototyping in winter 2025, then Wizard-of-Oz testing and recommendations in spring 2025. In the testing protocol, participants encountered simulated chatbot responses generated with ChatGPT, Claude, and DeepSeek without knowing which model produced a response.

Follow-up surveys explored preferences, perceived trust, and opportunities to improve. Figma prototypes and redesigned flows made the proposed experience concrete without requiring a deployed inference backend.

## Implementation

The public project page documents Figma for interactive prototyping, pdfplumber and Tesseract for examining existing documentation, and Google Forms and Sheets for feedback collection and analysis. The presentation site uses HTML, custom CSS, and JavaScript to organize project materials.

The technical recommendation centered on contextual, conversational help embedded in the user’s workflow. Supporting materials discuss privacy, deployment options, token use, visual aids, and integration considerations. These are planning and research deliverables, not evidence of production integration.

## Observations and outcomes

The team’s published summary reports preferences for brief, clear responses, contextual assistance, and transparent explanations. It describes favorable feedback on DeepSeek’s structured style within the prototype sessions. Without the underlying study records reviewed here, this portfolio does not infer statistical significance, a general model ranking, or quantified adoption improvements.

Completed public deliverables include the Expo poster, research presentation site, prototype links, final report links, Wizard-of-Oz materials, and stakeholder recommendations. Together they outline a possible roadmap for AgBizLogic’s future development.

## Limitations

The public page explicitly describes a research and design deliverable rather than a working software product. Simulated assistance cannot establish the reliability or safety of a deployed agricultural assistant. No production deployment, long-term adoption gain, or measured business impact is claimed.
